## 1. Two kinds of wallbox, and telling them apart (T020)

A wallbox on a hybrid inverter's bus, set up as the inverter's EV charger in iSolarCloud, is
**managed**: the inverter drives it, and writes to the wallbox's own registers are overwritten
within seconds (gerd's). A **standalone** wallbox (no inverter manages it; working mode EMS in its
own interface, as evcc needs) is controlled at its own registers. Nothing has been measured on a
standalone one yet.

Signs of a managed wallbox: the inverter's block (33540-33550) reads non-zero; the wallbox's start
mode (input 21314) reads 1, *start with EMS*; it answers through the dongle at unit 3; its 21203
moves with nothing written.

No register says which it is: input 21314 reads 1 idle and 3 charging on every wallbox read, and
the inverter's EV charger block reads its factory state on houses with no wallbox at all
([the wallbox](../.wiki/wiki/entities/device-wallbox.md)). Only behaviour does, and two things link
the two ends: `managing_entry`, where the dongle probe saw both entries, and
`SungrowRuntimeData.has_wallbox` on the inverter's side, which says whether one answers on its bus
(commit `0bcda31`) -- the same question asked from the other end.

**The division is the point: an inference decides what is shown and what auto repeat waits for;
the owner decides what is written.** A wrong yes writes registers an inverter overwrites.

Built (2026-11-19): the inference is `src/sungrow_modbus/wallbox/management.py`, fed the readings
observer's own polls at no extra read (`DiagnosisRunner._async_observe_wallbox`): 21203 moving
twice during a charge says managed, 30 charging polls without a move suggest standalone, an idle
wallbox says nothing. It is shown as *Diagnosis: test summary*'s `management` attribute with its
counts, and the owner is asked in **Configure** -- *Does no inverter manage this wallbox?*,
`CONF_WALLBOX_STANDALONE`, off by default -- which is what `declared_standalone` reads.
`tests/test_wallbox_management.py` holds the inference, the option, the wiring and that no
inference can declare a wallbox standalone by itself. Kept in memory only: a restart loses a few
minutes of the next charge, and the answer that must survive is the owner's.

**What is left**: a run against a standalone wallbox, which nobody has offered (section 11,
item 3).

## 2. Rules for any wallbox write

1. **Never write a managed wallbox's own registers.** The capability test refuses unless the
   wallbox is declared standalone (`--standalone`; Home Assistant never declares it yet), and
   refuses when 21203 moves between two reads with nothing written. Do not give `--standalone` at
   gerd's.
2. **Never write 21211 = 0, never offer under 6 A, never 0.**
3. **Snapshot first, restore after**, and restore the house, not just the register: after a mode
   change, write charging on if the car was charging before; then watch the car charge again.
4. **One client per path.** Home Assistant's polling is held off during a run; ask the owner and
   the maintainer whether anything else polls before testing.
5. **Write every finding into the day's raw note as soon as it is found**, before the next test.
6. The owner consents per phase of testing; the maintainer is asked before any push.

- **Status 8 is *disabled***: it reads 8 whenever 21211 is 0, and 1 (idle) again on enable.
- **A disable (21211 = 0) stops the charge, and writing 1 back does not restart it.** The owner had
  to enable it in the app and press Start.
- **The wallbox pauses on its own** when the home battery is at its minimum SoC (the app's *Grund*

- **A charging off reads back on for about 10 s** (2026-11-20, three stops in four, the owner's

  switch entity that reads it shows *on* for a poll.
- **Read-back after a write is not evidence** on this wallbox: it answered one read twice, and
  21203 reads 0 whenever no car charges. Judge by the car: status (input 21317), the phase currents

- **Two clients on the inverter's cable give timeouts**, and the TLS port (516) refuses connections
  opened in quick succession.

### 3.3 What others report on phase switching (web, 2026-11-14)

Register numbers here; evcc's code uses protocol addresses, one lower.

- **Sungrow switches automatically, by power.** Datasheets and dealers: automatic 1/3-phase
  switching with a Sungrow hybrid, charging from 1.38 kW of PV surplus. The iHomeManager's manual:
  when the charging power falls within **1.38-4.14 kW** the iHomeManager tells the charger to switch
  to single phase. No source names a setting, in iSolarCloud or elsewhere, to choose the phases by
  hand.
- **evcc on a standalone wallbox** (`charger/sungrow.go`, PR #23716 for issue #23715): writes
  holding **21204** (0 three, 1 single) only with the charge stopped: holding 21212 = 1 (stop),
  then 21204, then 21211 = 1, 21212 = 0 and the current; reads the phases in use at input 21270.
  Issue #23715: on an AC011E (firmware 1.7.186) a switch without a stop was ignored by some cars;
  evcc's maintainer calls a switch that does not interrupt the charge a firmware fault.
- **A fork's fix** (BastiSch/evcc, branch `fix_sungrow_phase_switching`, 2026-09-07) waits **5 s
  after the stop** before writing 21204. Its comment: the wallbox sets the phases back to three once
  it reaches *completed*, 2-3 s after a stop; and *the SH10RT does not stop the charger when it
  switches phases*: so the inverter switches phases on a managed wallbox, under load.
- **Cars differ**: a Toyota bZ4X needed a pause; a CUPRA Tavascan stopped at the three-phase
  minimum and did not resume on one phase (evcc discussions #23715, #28643).
- **Inverter and evcc on one wallbox fight** (evcc discussion #20739): users decouple the wallbox
  from the inverter for evcc to switch phases; one switched the wallbox to EMS mode for it.

So on gerd's managed wallbox the phases are the inverter's to switch, most likely in PV surplus
when the power wanted is 1.38-4.14 kW; section 9, test 5 watches for that.

Sungrow's own manuals, ha-modbus-manager and the 873-post Photovoltaikforum thread, read on
2026-11-14, agree: no setting anywhere, automatic only, and owners see one phase in **PV surplus
with energy purchase (33550) off**, three phase with it on. The wiki keeps it:
`.wiki/wiki/concepts/managed-wallbox-phases.md`.

## 4. What the capability tests cover

Three runners:

- **The managed test** (`src/sungrow_modbus/wallbox/managed_test.py`): writes the inverter's block,
  reads the wallbox, puts the house back. From a terminal,
  `device-diagnosis/scripts/python/sungrow_managed_wallbox_test.py INVERTER --wallbox WALLBOX`
  (a dry run; `--leave` writes, `--start-stop` adds the stop and start). In Home Assistant from the
  wallbox's own entry, in *Extended test* or *auto repeat*, when an inverter entry there manages it.
  Needs this tree's library until the release after 0.1.0a38.
- **The standalone test** (`src/sungrow_modbus/wallbox/capability_test.py`): writes the wallbox's
  own registers. Terminal only (`sungrow_wallbox_capability_test.py --standalone`); Home Assistant
  has its whole machinery but no option that declares a wallbox standalone.
- **The plausibility checks** (`src/sungrow_modbus/wallbox/plausibility.py`): read only, fed by
  auto repeat's tick on any wallbox entry.

## 5. One entry per address: the direct connection (T059)

A wallbox with an address of its own is an entry of its own and the only one that writes to it; an
inverter entry leaves it out by its serial (decided and built 2026-11-12: `doc/architecture.md`,
wiki `device-wallbox.md`). Left:

1. **The search's result says it.** Where the search finds an inverter behind a WiNet-S and a
   wallbox on 516, the list could say that the wallbox will be read on its own and not through the
   inverter. Today the inverter entry simply gives it up once the wallbox entry is added.
2. ~~**On hardware at gerd's**~~: done 2026-11-20 evening (wiki: `gerd-wallbox-2026-11-20-pairing`):
   through the WiNet-S the inverter entry gave the wallbox up to its own entry, took it back when that
   was deleted, and gave it up again when it was added.
3. ~~**The options screenshot**~~: retaken 2026-11-20 (`HA-settings-configure-capability-test.png`, the

Open, unchanged: Modbus over TLS on a **WiNet-S's** own 516 for the inverter. Worth building only
if 516 is a *separate* session pool from 502: hold 502 until it refuses, then try 516. The
certificate is settled (wiki: `device-winet-s.md`): one certificate is built into the firmware, its
key is on every unit, and verifying it gains nothing.

## 6. Which blocks identify a unit (T040)

**A second wallbox's diagnosis.** Settles whether 21211-21214 identify the unit. Three registers
wait on it: input 21201-21206 (the serial as Sungrow's portal shows it -- gerd's hashes to his
published stand-in, so this block is that serial; use it as the device identity?), input
21211-21214 (a short code, masked) and holding 21250-21259 (twenty constant ASCII digits, masked).
Whichever differ between two wallboxes identify a unit and stay masked; whichever match are a model
constant and can be unmasked.

A second AC22E-01 arrived 2026-11-10 (anonymous, #786), but both documents publish these blocks as
`null` with no stand-in per block, so they cannot be compared: settling it needs the masking to
publish a stand-in for each masked block (a hash compares without revealing), and a third reading.
The AC011E-01 at unit 4 arrived on 2026-11-16 (#786) and is ingested, but as a Home Assistant
diagnostics download, whose wallbox section carries none of the three blocks: 21211 and 21212
appear in it as the charger's enable and start/stop, not as the short code. So this still waits on
the masking change plus a reading that carries the blocks -- a terminal diagnosis of that wallbox
would do it.
